What does “15/70” mean?

Most movies used to be shot on 35mm film, which runs up and down through the camera. IMAX turned the film sideways. Each picture (called a frame) is 15 perforations long. Perforations are the little holes on the edge of the film. The camera uses 65mm film and the theater shows it on 70mm film, so that is where the name 15/70 comes from (“IMAX”).

≈3×bigger than a normal 70mm frame (my math, using frame sizes from “IMAX”)

A bigger frame means more detail, so the picture still looks sharp on a screen that is several stories tall.

The downside is that it uses a ton of film. A camera only holds about three minutes of film at a time (“IMAX”). The IMAX 70mm copy of Oppenheimer was 11 miles long and weighed about 600 pounds (O’Kane).

How IMAX started

IMAX started in Canada. In 1967, Graeme Ferguson, Roman Kroitor, Robert Kerr and William Shaw started the company that became IMAX. The first IMAX movie, Tiger Child, played at Expo 70 in Osaka, Japan. The first permanent IMAX theater opened in Toronto in 1971 (Acland).

The hardest part was the projector. Moving frames that big through a projector smoothly was a real problem. The answer was the “rolling loop,” invented by an Australian engineer named Ron Jones. It moves the film in a smooth, wave-like motion, which keeps the giant picture steady and in focus (Acland).

I think this is the most important invention behind IMAX, because without it the huge frames could not be shown at all.

How it changed movies

For a long time, IMAX movies were mostly documentaries. They played in places like the National Air and Space Museum in Washington, D.C. (Acland).

  • 2002
    IMAX started converting regular Hollywood movies, like Apollo 13, so they could play on IMAX screens (“IMAX”).
  • 2008
    The Dark Knight had about 28 minutes filmed with IMAX cameras (“IMAX”). After that, more big Hollywood movies started using them.

Not every change was good. Many newer IMAX theaters have much smaller screens than the original ones, so some people started calling them “LieMAX” (“IMAX”).

How directors use it

Most directors use IMAX cameras for their biggest scenes instead of the whole movie. Brad Bird filmed about 25 minutes of Mission: Impossible – Ghost Protocol in IMAX, and Michael Bay filmed about 10 minutes of Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen in IMAX (“IMAX”).

The reason is that the cameras are hard to work with. The old ones are so loud that it is hard to record people talking in quiet scenes (“IMAX”). So for years, filmmakers shot the talking scenes on quieter cameras and saved IMAX for the action (Leitner).

Christopher Nolan

Christopher Nolan is the director most connected to IMAX. Starting in 2008, he kept pushing it further.

  • 2008
    The Dark Knight: about 28 minutes in IMAX (“IMAX”).
  • 2012
    The Dark Knight Rises: 72 minutes in IMAX (“IMAX”).
  • 2023
    Oppenheimer: Kodak and FotoKem made the first black-and-white IMAX film just for this movie (O’Kane).
  • 2026
    The Odyssey: the first movie shot completely on IMAX film cameras (Leitner).

To make The Odyssey possible, IMAX built new cameras that are 30% quieter and have carbon-fiber bodies (Stout). The new camera is called the Keighley (Leitner). IMAX’s CEO said Nolan promised to make the movie “100 percent in IMAX” if they could fix the camera problems (Stout).

I think this shows that one director can actually change the technology itself.

What makes a theater IMAX?

A real IMAX theater is built around the screen. The classic screens are about 18 by 24 meters (59 by 79 feet) and are taller than normal movie screens. The seats are steep and close to the screen, so you are looking right at it (“IMAX”).

But not every IMAX is the same. Many IMAX theaters are digital and show a wider, shorter picture (1.90:1 instead of 1.43:1) (“IMAX”). Only a few can still play real 15/70 film. Oppenheimer played in 70mm IMAX in only about 30 theaters (O’Kane).

Answering my question

So what did 15/70 change?

Movies got bigger

IMAX went from museum documentaries to huge Hollywood movies, and in 2026 a whole movie was shot with it.

Directors changed how they film

Directors started planning their biggest scenes around IMAX cameras, even with the noise and the three-minute film loads.

The technology kept improving

Because filmmakers like Nolan kept asking for more, IMAX made new film and new, quieter cameras.

The limits

IMAX film is expensive and hard to use, and only a few theaters can still show it. A lot of theaters called IMAX show a smaller digital version instead.
